One capability set, several doors. Every operation exists once in the core and is exposed by thin adapters: REST under `/v1` with an OpenAPI 3.1 document, and MCP tools with the same names and the same schemas ([ADR-010](https://github.com/surfingdogai/inbox/blob/main/docs/adr/010-protocols.md)). Whichever door an agent takes, it gets the same item and the same errors.

The live OpenAPI document of our own instance: [inbox.surfingdog.ai/openapi.json](https://inbox.surfingdog.ai/openapi.json). Every instance serves its own at `/openapi.json`.

## Public operations

No authentication. Anonymous callers receive an `accessToken` when they create an item; agents with an agent key (`sdi_agent_…`) are recognised as a stable principal.

| Operation | REST | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `get_business_profile` | `GET /v1/business` | Name, time zone, currency, languages, the item types accepted, `price_negotiable` (`false`, out of the box: the business sets its prices, and a price of the customer's own goes to a person there; `true` once it takes a customer's own price as their answer, see below), `return_policy` (schema.org's `MerchantReturnPolicy`: `merchantReturnDays`, who pays to send things back in `returnFees`, a full refund) and, once the business filled it in, `trader`: who it is (`legal_name`, `address`, `country`, `email`, `phone`, `vat_id`, `complaints_url`). |
| `list_services` | `GET /v1/services` | Bookable services with duration and price: a fixed price is for the whole booking unless `price.per` is `person`; `withdrawal` says whether a customer who paid may withdraw (`standard`) or the exception the law allows (`dated_leisure`, `urgent_repair`, …). Paged. |
| `list_products` | `GET /v1/products` | Orderable products with prices in minor units, and `withdrawal` (`standard`, or `perishable`, `personalised`, `sealed_hygiene`, …). Paged, searchable with `q`. |
| `check_availability` | `GET /v1/availability` | Free start times for a service between `from` and `to` (at most 14 days). None that has started, and none that starts within the business's minimum notice (an hour unless it sets another). `party_size` is accepted and does not change the answer yet: a booking takes one place whatever its size. |
| `request_quote` | `POST /v1/quotes` | Ask for a price on something custom. Creates a `quote_request`. |
| `create_booking` | `POST /v1/bookings` | Request a service at a time. Check availability first. Creates a `booking`, at the business's price for a fixed-price service, times `partySize` for a service priced per person. A priced booking takes the confirm step first: see [The confirm step](#the-confirm-step). A time that has started is refused (`422` on `payload.startTime`, "That time has passed."); one inside the minimum notice is taken and waits for the business, which does not propose or quote a time inside it either. |
| `create_order` | `POST /v1/orders` | Order products. Creates an `order`, at the business's price for every line that names a product. A priced order takes the confirm step first. |
| `get_item_status` | `GET /v1/items/{id}` | The current state of an item you created, in the business's words and language: what it proposed and waits for you to answer (`offer`), who it waits on (`waiting_on`), what you can do next (`next`), a six-character `reference`, the conversation (`thread`): the business's replies and your messages, and for a booking or an order `withdrawal` and `refunds` (see [Returns and withdrawal](#returns-and-withdrawal)). |
| `accept_offer` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/accept`, or `POST /v1/items/{id}/offers/{offer}/accept` | Accept another time the business proposed for a booking, its quote, its changes to an order, or a change it asks for to a confirmed booking or an accepted order (`offer.kind: change`). Takes the confirm step: send the `terms_sha` your person said yes to, and the offer's id (`offer_id`, or in the path), so an answer to one since replaced does nothing. See [Answering the business](#answering-the-business). |
| `decline_offer` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/decline`, or `POST /v1/items/{id}/offers/{offer}/decline` | Decline it: a booking request or an order whose proposed time or changes you decline is closed, as the customer chose; a declined quote closes the request; a declined change keeps the booking or order as agreed, and on a change your person asked for (`requested_change`) it takes it back. Optional `reason`, and `reason_code` (`price_sensitivity`, `timing_deferred`, `quantity`, `delivery`, `returns_policy`, `other`). |
| `make_offer` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/offers` | Answer with your person's own terms, only what they would change (`terms`): another `start_time` (a booking, one of the free times; a quote request, the time it is for), other quantities for the lines of changes the business suggested to an order (`lines: [{index, quantity}]`, 0 drops one), another `delivery_when`, or how many (`quantity`) for a quote request. It goes back to the business as their request. On a confirmed booking or an accepted order it asks for a change to what was agreed: see [Changing what was agreed](#changing-what-was-agreed). A price of their own (`total_price`, `unit_price`), a `note` alone, or a suggestion past the business's last round, goes to a person there as their message: `202` with `passed_on`, never refused, and what the business proposed still stands. |
| `suggest_time` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/counter` | Ask for another time than the one proposed: `start_time`, one of the free times `check_availability` lists. The booking goes back to the business. On a confirmed booking, ask to move it. The same as `make_offer` with `terms.start_time`. |
| `provide_details` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/details` | Answer what the business asked (`details`): an item waiting on your details moves on (`200`); on any other item they are kept as your message (`202`, `waiting_on: "us"`). |
| `withdraw_from_contract` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/withdraw` | "Withdraw from contract here": your person withdraws from a booking they paid for or an order, in two steps. See [Returns and withdrawal](#returns-and-withdrawal). |
| `request_return` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/returns` | Once an order's goods reached your person: ask to send them back (`reason`: `faulty`, `not_as_described`, `wrong_item`, `changed_mind` or `other`; `lines`; `wants`; `note`). `201` with the return in `linked`. |
| `cancel_item` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/cancel` | Cancel an item you created. After the business's cancellation window, a confirmed booking is cancelled late where the business records late cancellations, and refused where it does not. A booking your person paid for, or an order paid for and not yet sent, is cancelled as their withdrawal while the period runs: never late, and what they paid is refunded. On a return, it drops the return while the goods are still with your person; once they are back with the business, only a person there can. |
| `send_message` | `POST /v1/messages` | Start a conversation, or reply on an item you own with `item_id`. A reply on an item waiting on your details answers them: the item moves on. |
| `acknowledge_receipt` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/receipt-ack` | Counter-sign a receipt on your item with your agent's own Ed25519 key, so both sides hold it; or, in a request you sign, name it by `receipt_id`. See [Receipts](/docs/receipts/). |
| `verify_customer` | `POST /v1/customers/verify` | Prove you are a customer the business already knows: six digits go to the address it has for you (`202 {sent_to}`), and sending them back as `code` recognises you (`200 {recognised: "strong"}`). On a test item nothing is sent (`202 {sent_to, test: true}`): the code is left on the item for the business to read. |

Create calls take `payload` (the typed fields, schema.org names in camelCase), an optional `contact` (`name`, `email`, `phone`, `locale`), an optional free-text `message`, and `idempotency_key`.

**The business sets its prices.** A booking of a service priced `fixed`, and every order line that names a product (`productId`, or else `sku`), costs what the business's catalogue says; an order's `totalPrice` is then its lines' prices times their quantities. A different price in the request does not change that: it is kept on the item as `customerStatedPrice` (on the order, and on each line it differs for), for the owner to see, and rules never read it. What the catalogue prices it also names: the service's name, and the line's product's `productId`, `sku` and `name`, replace the request's. A line whose `productId` and `sku` name two different products, and an order whose total is too large to write down exactly, are refused with `422`. A service priced per person (`price.per: "person"`) costs its price times the booking's `partySize` (one person when it has none). A line naming no product the business has, and a service priced `from`, by `quote` or not at all, keep the price written. Those, and a fixed-price service booked for longer than it lasts, wait for the business to price them: nothing confirms or accepts them automatically. A custom job is a `request_quote`.

**Who you carry.** Every public call takes `pass` (a person's passes, space-separated, at most 8) and `key` (a person's key, exchanged once for a pass), or the passes in an `Sdi-Pass` header; a `GET` reads only the header. Never put either in a URL. The inbox presents each to the network that issued it, when that network is switched on, and stores none of them: only a hash of a pass, to recognise it again. Create and status answers carry `identity`:

| Field | |
|---|---|
| `recognised` | `strong` (a customer the business knows, proven), `weak` (the email or phone of one, not proven) or `none`. |
| `passes` | Passes to keep, `[{network, pass}]`: a first booking's pass, or the one a key became. Shown in the answer that made them. |
| `verify` | `available` when a one-time code can prove a weak match; `sent_to`, the masked address, once one went out. |
| `networks` | What happened at each network: `issued`, `presented`, `person_exists`, `pending`, `unknown_pass`, `revoked`, `pass_requires_signature`, `carries_secret`, `unreachable`, `rate_limited`, `not_enabled`, `cannot_sign`. |
| `guide` | `https://surfingdog.ai/for-agents.md` |

A first booking or order with an email and nothing carried asks each network that issues for a key, which is emailed to the customer a day later, alone, with a line about the booking network and a link to a page that explains it, where they can switch it off for themselves; nothing about the booking waits for it. A customer who switched it off (or whom the business switched it off for) is never asked about again: no key, no presentation, no receipt or acknowledgement goes to any network about them, nothing a network said about them is read, and their passes still recognise them, from their hash, with no network call; `identity.networks` is then empty. A network that is slow or down leaves the customer `new` there, never refused. How an agent should use all this is [the agent guide](https://surfingdog.ai/for-agents.md).

**Signed agents.** An agent may sign any request with HTTP Message Signatures (`sdi-agent/1`, Web Bot Auth compatible: its own key in `Sdi-Agent-Key`, or a platform's key named by `Signature-Agent`). An unsigned request is served the same; a signature that does not verify counts as unsigned, and the answer says why in `Sdi-Signature: invalid; reason="…"`. A signed request that changes something is kept once: the same signature again is a retry when it comes from the same client with the same idempotency key (answered as the first time), and `401 replayed_signature` otherwise. A pass reference (`sdpass1_<network>_<id>`) works only in a request signed by a key delegated to it: the inbox forwards that signature to the network, which checks it again, so a signed request must not carry a secret in its URL or its `Sdi-Pass`, nor cover any header but `Content-Digest`, `Content-Type`, `Sdi-Pass` and its key's (`Sdi-Agent-Key` or `Signature-Agent`) (the answer says `carries_secret` and nothing is forwarded). Send an access token in `X-Access-Token` on a signed request. `@surfingdog/sdk` signs requests for you.

## Answering the business

When the business proposes another time for a booking, sends a quote, or suggests changes to an order (another quantity, a price of its own, a delivery date), it waits for the customer. The status (`GET /v1/items/{id}`, `get_item_status`) then carries:

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| `offer` | `id` (name it when you answer; `null` for one made before offers had ids, until the inbox gives it one), `kind` (`time`, `quote`, `order`, or `change` for a change the business asks for to a confirmed booking or an accepted order), `terms` (`startTime`, `endTime`, `partySize`, `totalPrice`; for a quote `lines`, `notes`, `validThrough`, `creates`; for changes to an order `lines`, `totalPrice`, `delivery`; for a change, the booking or order as it would be), `terms_sha` (the fingerprint of those terms), `deadline` (until when you can answer: see Deadlines below), `obligation_to_pay`, `human` (the terms in the business's words), `changes` (what differs from what it answers or replaces, as paths into `terms`: `$.startTime`, `$.lines[0].quantity`), `warnings` (`{type: "warning", code: "price_changed", severity: "requires_buyer_review"}` whenever money changed: make sure your person saw the new price) `disclosures` (`held` or `unheld` for a time: kept for them until they answer, or theirs if still free; `withdrawable` when the business may still withdraw it; `personalised_price` when its price was chosen for them) and `expired` (its deadline has passed, before the business's sweep closes it: it can no longer be accepted, and `next` no longer offers `accept_offer`). `null` when nothing waits for an answer. |
| `agreed` | The terms both sides last agreed and their `terms_sha`; `null` before anything was. |
| `requested_change` | A change your person asked for to a confirmed booking or an accepted order, while the business has not answered: `terms` (as it would be), `terms_sha` and `until` (when it lapses). What was agreed stands meanwhile; `null` otherwise. |
| `waiting_on` | `you` (a time, a quote, changes or a change to answer, details asked for, a payment), `us` (the business has it), or `null` once it is closed. |
| `next` | What the customer can do now: `accept_offer`, `decline_offer`, `suggest_time` (a time, or another time for a confirmed booking), `make_offer` (a quote, changes to an order, a change to an accepted order), `provide_details`, `cancel_item`, `send_message`, each with the business's label. |
| `reference` | Six characters the customer can quote back. |
| `thread` | The conversation, oldest first, the last fifty: `from` (`you`, the customer; `us`, the business), `text`, `at`, and `automated: true` on what a rule or the business's AI sent. The business's internal notes are never in it. |

The item seen by its customer carries none of the business's own flags, in any answer (a create, the status door, an answer to an offer, a cancellation, a message), and a message the business put aside as spam reads as `closed`. Every sentence (`human`, `offer.human`) is the business's, in its language (the customer's `contact.locale`, else the business's first language), with every time in the business's time zone and the zone named.

**The confirm step.** A booking or an order that carries a price binds the customer — a consumer, unless the business sells only to businesses (`commerce.customers`) — only once they confirmed its summary, as the law asks before an order to pay (CRD art. 8(2)). So `create_booking` and `create_order` without `terms_sha` write nothing and answer `409 confirm_terms`: `details.summary` says what, when, from whom (the business's legal name and address, once it gave them), the total, the right of withdrawal or why there is none, and that confirming means paying; `details.terms_sha` is its fingerprint and `details.obligation_to_pay` is `true`. Show your person the summary and send the same request again with that `terms_sha` on their clear yes: it is then a first request, whatever idempotency key it carries, and their request binds them. With the fingerprint of other terms (the business changed a price meanwhile) the answer is the same `409` with the current summary and `details.changed: true`. A request that holds no price of the business's yet (a line naming no product, a service priced `from` or by quote) or a free one needs no confirm step; the business's own offer, when it prices it, takes one. On MCP the question is an ordinary result: its text starts "Nothing is sent yet" and `structuredContent.confirm` holds the details.

Accepting binds the customer too; when the terms carry a price, to pay it. So `accept_offer` without `terms_sha` writes nothing and answers `409 confirm_terms`, whose `details` hold `summary` (what to show your person), `terms_sha` and `obligation_to_pay`. Show them the summary; accept again with that `terms_sha` only on their clear yes. Different terms, or an `offer_id` the business has since replaced, are `409 offer_changed` with the current `offer`; an offer past its deadline is `410 offer_expired`; a time that is no longer free is `409 slot_taken`, and one that has started `409 guard_failed` (`not_too_soon`). An accepted time confirms the booking; accepted changes accept the order on them. An accepted quote creates what it was for in the same write, already promised: the booking confirmed at the quoted time with its place held, or the order accepted, returned as `linked`. Nothing is ever booked in the past.

**Answering with your own terms.** `make_offer` (and `suggest_time`, for a time) sends back only what your person would change; the item goes back to the business as their request, which it takes, answers with another offer, or lets lapse. Out of the box the business sets its prices (`price_negotiable: false`), so a price of their own is not an answer: it goes to a person at the business as their message (`202`, `passed_on` in the business's words), and what the business proposed still stands until its deadline. So does a suggestion after a few rounds of back and forth (`negotiation.maxRounds`, 3 unless the business sets another, counting the request as the first): never refused, answered by a person.

**A price of their own.** Where the business takes one (`price_negotiable: true`, its `negotiation.priceCounters`), `make_offer` may carry it: `total_price` for a time it proposed (with or without another `start_time`), `unit_price` on the lines of changes it suggested to an order, in the business's currency (`422` otherwise). It is their answer, back with the business, which takes it, answers it, or leaves it; taking it binds them to pay it, as any acceptance does. It still goes to a person as their message, never refused, on a quote, on a product or service the business does not haggle, while the customer has more open negotiations on price than the business allows (`negotiation.perCustomer.open`, 3), after as many prices of their own for the same product or service in `perCustomer.days` (30) as it allows (`perCustomer.priceCounters`, 3), and past the last round.

**A price for them.** The business may give a customer whose record with it earned one a better price: a reward it set, applied by the inbox, never below the business's lowest price and never above its list price. That price is the one in `terms`, in the confirm step's summary and in every offer; its `human` sentence, the summary and the emails say it was personalised by automated decision-making, with the list price beside it (`disclosures` holds `personalised_price`, and the payload's `personalised.listPrice` the list price). A price your person states never becomes the price: the list price you write for a rewarded customer is read as theirs.

**Deadlines.** Nothing waits forever. A request with the business lapses unanswered after `booking.autoExpireHours` (a booking, 72 unless set; never later than its start) or `negotiation.counterValidHours` (an order or a quote request, 72), counted from when it came in or the customer last answered or was asked something; what the business proposed lapses at its `deadline`: a time a person proposed at its start less the minimum notice, a quote at its `validThrough`, changes to an order at the date they give, and anything with no date, or proposed by the business's AI or a rule, `negotiation.offerValidHours` (48) after it was made at the latest. A deadline is checked in the write that would take it (`410 offer_expired`), and the inbox's sweep closes what lapsed within a quarter of an hour (`expired`) and tells the customer in the business's words. A request made before this release, or one the business wrote down itself (the owner, a shop's system, an integration key), keeps no clock and never lapses on its own until the customer next answers. When the business takes a request whose clock ran out before the sweep reached it, it goes back to the customer as the business's offer on the same terms, which they accept: late, never refused.

## Changing what was agreed

A confirmed booking or an accepted order can change without being cancelled, from either side. **Your person asks** with `make_offer` (or `suggest_time`): another `start_time` for a booking, one the business would offer (the booking's own places count as free to it; the end keeps its length), or, for an order, other quantities of its lines (`lines: [{index, quantity}]`, by index in the item's `orderedItem`, 0 drops one) or another `delivery_when`; never a price. The answer is `200` with the item: it stays as agreed, and `requested_change` shows what waits for the business, until it lapses (`negotiation.counterValidHours`, and before either start). `decline_offer` takes it back. When the business accepts it the promise moves, and your person is told; when it cannot, or it lapses, it stays as agreed. **The business asks**: the status carries an `offer` of `kind: change` (the booking or the order as it would be, until when to answer, `held` when the new time is kept for them), and `accept_offer` with its `terms_sha` takes it — `obligation_to_pay` only when it asks more of them than was agreed — while `decline_offer` keeps it as it is. It lapses at its deadline, and the promise stays as agreed. If it can no longer be made online by the time your person says yes — payment was asked for since and it would change the order's total, or a network the business reports to now holds the promise under rules before version 6 — their yes goes to a person at the business as their message (`202`, `passed_on`), never refused, and what was agreed stands until that person answers. A change accepted is recorded as a signed receipt of its own (`kind: amended`, on the item's `receipts`, with the new dates and the fingerprint of what was agreed), which your agent may counter-sign like any receipt: see [Receipts](/docs/receipts/).

A change goes to a person at the business as your person's message (`202`, `passed_on`, "what we agreed stands"), never refused, when the promise has had the changes it may have (`negotiation.changes.maxPerItem`, three at most), would move more than 90 days from what was first agreed, is past the last round of asking, would change an order's total once payment was asked for or made, or is held by a network the business reports to, or about to be sent to one, whose rules in force are older than version 6. Its email carries **Accept the change** and **Keep it as it is** links; every confirmation of a booking carries **Change the time**, which opens the free times.

**Holds.** With `booking.holdOnPropose` on, a time the business proposes is kept for the customer until they answer: nobody else can book it meanwhile, and their acceptance takes it; at most `booking.maxHolds` (2) held at once per customer, after which a proposed time goes out unheld, theirs if still free.

**Emails.** The customer hears from the business by email too, in its name and in the customer's language (English or Portuguese: the customer's `contact.locale`, else the business's first language): an acknowledgement a moment after a booking, order or quote request arrives (a request a rule answers at once gets the answer instead), and an email for every change they need to know of, each with the time in the business's zone and the zone named, the price and the total, until when to answer, and a six-character reference. A reply to one of them lands on the same item: every email about an item names its anchor first in `References`, and the email door matches `In-Reply-To` and `References` against it, against each email's own id, and against the id the mail service gave it. An email no person at the business typed — a rule's, the business's AI's, another system's (an integration key, a shop's connector) — ends with one line: "This reply was sent automatically. Reply to reach a person." A system whose request says a person wrote it (`written_by: "person"` on `reply` or `transition_item`, honoured from an integration key) is not; the owner's AI always is. Every email asks mailboxes not to answer it by themselves (`X-Auto-Response-Suppress: OOF, AutoReply`), and one no person at the business caused says so (`Auto-Submitted: auto-replied` for a reply or the acknowledgement, `auto-generated` otherwise, where the mail service accepts that header; one that refuses it gets the email without it). An out-of-office or a returned email never becomes an item and is never answered: one that answers an item is kept on it as a note, and any other is dropped. The acknowledgement goes to one address at most three times a day, since anyone can make a request with any address; one held back is kept as not sent (`ack_limit`). A test item (`sandbox`) emails nobody, customer or owner: its emails are kept as not sent (`test_item`). The owner is told of new requests at `notifications.ownerEmail`, else at the address the first owner signs in with; with neither, the email is kept as not sent (`no_address`).

The same answers reach a customer by email: the business's email carries **Accept**, **Decline** and **Pick another time** links (for changes to an order, **Accept** and **Decline**; or **Send the details**), each opening a small page the instance serves at `/c/{token}` in the business's name and the customer's language. Opening a link only shows the terms; the page's button acts, once. A link names one item and one answer, is signed with the instance's `INBOX_SECRET_KEY`, and lives until the "answer by" date its email shows, and a day more (a quote's, until it lapses and a day more; a details link, 14 days): opened after that date, the page says why it is too late, and offers the free times. Without `INBOX_SECRET_KEY`, or without a public URL (`INBOX_PUBLIC_URL`, else the app URL in the notification settings), the email asks for a reply instead, and the assistant's door works as ever.

## Returns and withdrawal

A customer may withdraw from a contract made at a distance within the period the law gives, 14 days (or the business's longer `returns.days`), without giving a reason (ADR-018 §7): from an order, from its acceptance until 14 days after the goods reached them; from a booking they paid for (`record_payment`; an unpaid booking is a reservation, which they simply cancel), from the booking until it starts or the period ends. The period is counted as the law counts it: the day it starts from is not counted, and one that would end on a weekend or a public holiday (Portugal's, or England and Wales's, by `commerce.legal.country`) ends at the end of the next working day, in the business's time zone. Nothing excepted may be withdrawn from (`withdrawal` on the product or service: made to order, perishable, for a set date, …), and a business that sells only to businesses (`commerce.customers`) gives no such right.

`GET /v1/items/{id}` says where it stands: `withdrawal` is `{available, until, label}` (`until` is `null` while the goods are on their way: the customer may withdraw already), and when there is none, `why` (`excepted`, with the business's words in `reason`; `lapsed`; `business_customer`; `not_paid`; `started`). `next` offers `withdraw_from_contract` while it runs, and `request_return` once the goods reached them.

**Withdrawing** (`POST /v1/items/{id}/withdraw`, `withdraw_from_contract`) takes two steps, as the law asks. Without `confirm_withdrawal` nothing is sent and the answer is `409 confirm_withdrawal`, whose `details.statement` holds what to show your person: `text` (the statement, "I withdraw from my contract for …"), `name`, `email` (where their copy goes), `contract`, `reference`, `until`, `available`, `label` ("Withdraw from contract here", or the words of the law the business sells under) and `confirm` (the button, "Confirm withdrawal"). With `confirm_withdrawal: true` (and `lines` to send back only some of the goods, `note`): before the goods went out, the order or the booking ends, neutrally — never a late cancellation — and what was paid is owed back within 14 days, as a refund in `linked`; once they reached your person, the order stands and the goods come back within 14 days, the refund following them. The business emails a copy at once. It is never refused: past the period, or for something excepted, it becomes a return under the business's own policy once the goods reached them (`200`, the return in `linked`), and otherwise your person's message for a person there (`202`, `passed_on`, saying why). A booking or an order in its first states (a request) has nothing to withdraw from: `409 wrong_state`, cancel it instead.

**Returns** (`POST /v1/items/{id}/returns`, `request_return`) are for goods that reached your person: `faulty`, `not_as_described` and `wrong_item` are faulty goods under the legal guarantee (sending them back costs nothing), answered by the business within its `returns.respondHours`; `changed_mind` within the period is a withdrawal, agreed at once; after it, a return under the business's own policy, which it answers. One return of an order is open at a time (`409 guard_failed`, `no_open_return`): write to add to it. Each return is an item of type `refund`, the same customer's (the order's access token reads and cancels it), with `kind` (`withdrawal`, `faulty`, `policy`, and `cancellation` or `price_adjustment` for the business's own), `amount` (what is owed: what was paid for the lines that come back), `goodsBack`, `returnBy`, `refundDue` once nothing more has to come back, and its states: `requested`, `approved`, `goods_received`, `refunded`, `rejected`, `cancelled`. The order or booking lists them in `refunds`, each with its reference and the business's sentence.

**Refunds.** A withdrawal is refunded within 14 days of the notice, or three days after the goods, or proof of sending them, arrive when that is later; anything else `returns.refundDays` (14 at most) after nothing more has to come back. The amount is all of what is owed or more, up to what was paid: anything less needs the customer's agreement (`409 guard_failed`, `refund_amount`). The owner is told three days before a refund falls due, or, in Portugal, from the tenth day after a withdrawal, since a late one is owed twice over there. A charge-back while a return is open, or after a refund paid late, records nothing against the customer. Returning something never counts in what the business knows of a customer.

## Keys and scopes

Give every system that calls the inbox its own key: Zapier, a shop, a till, a form plugin. Create one in the owner app under **Settings → Keys**, or with `POST /v1/owner/api-keys`:

```json
{ "name": "Zapier", "preset": "automation" }
```

The answer holds the key (`sdi_own_…`) once. Send it as `Authorization: Bearer …`. It works on `/v1/owner` and `/mcp/owner` until it is revoked or reaches its optional `expires_at`.

| Preset | Scopes | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `automation` | `inbox:read inbox:write events:read` | Zapier, Make, n8n |
| `shop_sync` | `catalogue:write inbox:read inbox:write events:read` | A shop or a point of sale |
| `calendar_sync` | `availability:write inbox:read inbox:write events:read` | A calendar or a booking tool |
| `read_only` | `inbox:read events:read settings:read` | Reports and dashboards |

The scopes are `inbox:read`, `inbox:write`, `events:read`, `catalogue:write`, `availability:write`, `settings:read`, `settings:write`, `setup:run` (rules), `integrations:write` (webhooks and feeds), `keys:write`, `customers:erase` (erase one customer) and `money:write` (record payments and refunds, and price offers and quotes, as your shop or till does). The last two are in no preset: only the owner gives them, and an AI app is never offered them. OAuth clients ask for the same names, but for those two. A key never carries `keys:write`: an integration key cannot mint keys. A key without `money:write` is held to your limits as your AI is: it records no payment, failed payment, charge-back or refund and gives no payment link (`403 not_allowed`, `money_recorded`), and what it offers is judged against your limits. Keys that could move items along before the limits existed (`inbox:write`, or everything) were given `money:write` when the instance was upgraded, so they keep working as they did.

**The owner's AI and returns.** It may open a return (`open_return`), record a withdrawal naming the customer's message (`record_withdrawal` with `entryId`), and approve a return inside the owner's policy — faulty goods, or within `returns.days` with nothing excepted — with the goods coming back, while `negotiation.ai.mayAuthorizeReturnsInPolicy` is on (the default; `409 guard_failed`, `return_allowed` or `return_outside_policy`, `draft_for_owner`). Refusing a return, disputing what came back, cancelling a paid order (`403 not_allowed`, `person_only`), agreeing a refund with nothing sent back (`owner_money`), recording a payment or the refund itself and changing `returns` or `commerce` are the owner's. A product's or service's `withdrawal` is the owner's (or staff's) to set, never the AI's or another system's key (`403 not_allowed`, `owner_only`); feeds never write it.

**The owner's AI and money.** The owner's AI — an AI app the owner connected over OAuth (actor `owner_ai`), or anything that comes through the owner's MCP, even with a full owner key — confirms and proposes times, and does with money only what [your limits](#your-limits) let it. It never records a payment, a failed payment, a charge-back or a refund, asks for payment with a link of its own (`paymentUrl`), changes a service's or product's price or the business's currency, publishes a priced product or service, connects or disconnects a feed, or writes, changes or switches on a rule that sends a quote, or whose words name an amount or something off a price (`403 not_allowed`, reason `owner_money`; nor does a key without `money:write`); it cannot confirm a booking or accept an order whose request held a price the catalogue does not give (`409 guard_failed`, `business_priced`). A booking or order it makes through the public doors is priced from the catalogue, as a customer's is. A product, or a service with a price, that it adds is saved unpublished (`active: false`) for the owner to check and publish. Each refusal carries `details.draft_for_owner: true`: the AI leaves the owner a note (`reply` with `internal: true`) with what it suggests. Changes to what was agreed follow the owner's settings (`negotiation.ai`): it accepts a customer's change to a free time only while `mayAcceptChanges` is on (the default), before `negotiation.changes.customerCutoffMin` (else the cancellation window) and, for an order, at the catalogue's prices (`409 guard_failed`, `change_allowed` or `business_priced`, `draft_for_owner`); what it asks for is an offer, judged by your limits. While the cancellation window is also that cutoff (no `customerCutoffMin` of the owner's), the AI may lengthen `booking.cancellationWindowMin` but never shorten it (`403 not_allowed`, `owner_in_person`); likewise it, or a key, may lower `orders.maxValueWithoutApprovalMinor` but never raise it or set it to none. What it proposes holds at most `negotiation.offerValidHours`, and says so (a quote or changes it sends with a later date carry that one instead).

**The owner's AI** — an app connected over OAuth, or anything on the owner MCP, whatever key it holds — reads what customers write, and a customer can write instructions. So nothing that changes where the inbox sends its data is the AI's, whatever its scopes: it gets `403 not_allowed` with `details.reason: "owner_in_person"` and `details.ask_owner: true` for creating or revoking a key, adding, changing, rotating or removing a webhook endpoint (pausing one, `active: false`, is allowed), changing `notifications.ownerEmail`, `notifications.appUrl`, `email.fromAddress`, `email.fromName` or `email.replyTo`, switching webhooks or test mode on, and for everything below. The owner in person does these, and so may an integration key that holds `integrations:write` (endpoints) or `settings:write` (those settings) by name. Only the owner in person changes `negotiation` (how long offers hold, the rounds automation may go, whether what the business proposes binds it), `returns` (the return policy), `commerce` (who the business sells to, and who it is), `email.inboundSecret`, `integrations.webhooks.allowPrivateTargets`, `identity`, `customers.otp` and the `security` section, or switches a network on, lets it issue codes or shares more with it: a network is sent customers' email addresses. A document written back with those unchanged is accepted and leaves them as they are. `security.aiMayCreateKeys` is retired and grants nothing. A reply or a transition note from the AI that names another customer's email address or phone number, or carries a key or secret, is refused too (`details.reason: "would_leak"`). What customers wrote reaches the AI after each tool's own text, in a block drawn with a random boundary, and in `structuredContent.untrusted_content`.

**Scopes are logged first.** In this release a call outside a key's scopes, or outside an AI app's granted scopes, still goes through, and it is recorded: `list_api_keys` shows each key's calls outside its scopes, with the scope it needed, and the same for AI apps. The owner can refuse them now with **Refuse calls outside a key's scopes** (`security.enforceScopes`); a later release turns that on for everyone. An AI that sets things up should ask for the scopes it needs when it connects.

**Limits.** An integration key has a generous bucket of its own, about ten calls a second, so a loop between the inbox and another system cannot run for ever. The owner's own session, keys and AI are not limited.

## Your limits

What your AI, your rules and an integration key without `money:write` may agree to on their own is yours to set, in code rather than in a prompt (`negotiation.ai`: in the owner app, **Settings → Prices, and what your AI may agree to**, and each product's or service's lowest price under **Settings → Services**). Out of the box it is time yes, money no: any free time within seven days of the one asked for, and no price but the customer's.

| Limit | Breach | Out of the box |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A catalogue line under your lowest price for it (`GET`/`PUT /v1/owner/catalogue/floors`), or under the customer's price less `maxDiscountPct` | `below_floor` | no discount |
| A catalogue line above the customer's price: a surcharge, or holding back a reward | `above_list` | always |
| Answering a customer's own price with another: automation takes theirs, or leaves it to a person | `counter_priced` | always |
| A price the catalogue does not give: a quote, a line of its own, a longer booking (`mayPriceCustom`); or keeping a price the customer's request set; or taking a customer's own price for what a person priced | `custom_line` | never |
| A time further than `maxTimeShiftMin` from the one asked for, or one you would not offer (outside the opening hours, on a closed day, off the grid) | `time_moved` | 7 days |
| A delivery later than `maxDelayDays` after the one asked for | `delivery_later` | 0 days |
| Dearer for the customer than what you last offered them in this negotiation | `worse_than_before` | always |
| Past the last round (`negotiation.maxRounds`) | `rounds_exhausted` | 3 |
| Asking a customer for a change to what was agreed (`mayProposeChanges`) | `change_not_allowed` | never |
| An order above the value you accept in person (`orders.maxValueWithoutApprovalMinor`) | `over_approval_value` | none |
| A note, a reply or the name of a line, naming an amount of money you have not offered (a catalogue price counts only beside the name of what it prices), in figures or in words, or something off a price (a percentage off, half price, free of charge) | `amount_named` | always |

An **offer** outside them — a time, a quote, changes to an order, a change to what was agreed — is not sent: it is kept as the item's **draft** (`202`, `drafted: {id, breaches}`), the item is marked for a person, and you are emailed at most once an hour per item. `get_item` and `list_offers` show it with its breaches and whether the item has moved since (`stale`); you in person send it as it is (with the notice beside a price the inbox chose for the customer), make your own offer, or drop it; neither your AI nor a key sends or drops one. A newer draft replaces the last. An **acceptance** outside them (`confirm`, `accept`: a customer's own price under the floor or above the list price, an order above the value you accept in person) is refused, `422 outside_limits` with `details.breaches`. A **reply** naming money you have not offered is kept as an internal note (`202`, `held`); a note with a transition that is not an offer is refused as an acceptance is. Breaches are codes, never numbers: no error, draft or sentence tells automation where a limit is, so no customer's message can get it to recite one. Refunding with nothing to send back is automation's only up to `maxRefundMinor` for an order, counting what was already agreed on it with nothing back (none out of the box: `refund_over_max`, else `owner_money`), and approving a return outside your policy is yours (`return_outside_policy`).

You in person are never held: your offers go as you make them, at the prices you type. Only you change `negotiation`, the floors, and whether a product or service may be haggled (`negotiable` on `upsert_product` and `upsert_service`, `403 not_allowed` for anyone else; feeds never write it).

**Rewards.** A better price for a customer whose record earned it, set by you, applied by the inbox (`negotiation.rewards`, keyed by a name of yours): `{"regulars": {"if": {"path": "customer.completed", "op": "gte", "value": 3}, "pct": 5, "only": null, "says": "Thank you for coming back."}}`. `if` is a rule condition that reads only what the customer earned — your own history with them (`customer_known`, `customer.*`) and the standing their assistant showed (`person_trusted`, `person_tier_on`, `person.*`), combined with `all` and `any`; `not`, how sure the inbox is of who they are (`customer.match`), what they have open now (`customer.open_bookings`), anything else, and anything about where someone lives or comes from are refused when saved (`422`, the field to fix), and so is a condition a customer with no record here would meet ("no no-shows", "not trusted"): a reward only ever lifts, so it is never a price for everyone but those with something against them. A request the inbox priced with a reward stays at that price if you change the rewards before it is confirmed. `pct` is 1 to 50 off the list price, rounded to the cent in the customer's favour, never below your lowest price; `only` names the products and services it covers (`null`, all); `says` is a line for the customer after the notice, checked for words a customer should not read from you. The best matching reward applies, never several. Every price a reward or automation chose below the list price carries the personalised-price notice. A rule that reads a customer's standing still only confirms, accepts, asks for payment, flags or replies; one that reads where they live or come from may not set a price or terms (`422 geo_terms`). No rule reads your limits, your rewards or a secret from the settings: what a rule can read, a test of it can tell your AI.

## Conventions

**Idempotency.** Every write takes an idempotency key: in the `Idempotency-Key` header or as `idempotency_key` in the body on REST, as the `idempotency_key` argument on MCP. That includes the owner's setup writes (services, products, hours, rules, settings, webhooks, feeds, keys). Same key and same request: the stored answer is replayed with status `200` and `Idempotent-Replayed: true`, and nothing is done twice. Same key and a different request: `422 idempotency_mismatch`. The owner's and the owner's AI's keys are scoped to the business, so a retry through MCP of a request first sent through REST is the same request; an integration key's keys are its own, so two systems that pick the same key never collide; a customer's keys are scoped to the customer and the door. An answer that carries a secret (a signing secret, a key) is stored sealed, and a replay shows the secret only to whoever made the first request. Keys are kept for 30 days: they are for retries, not a record.

**Who did it.** Every event, in the history, the [event stream and webhooks](/docs/webhooks/), carries `actor` (`kind`: `owner`, `owner_ai`, `integration`, `connector`, `rule`, `system`, `customer_agent` or `customer_human`; `id`; and `name` for a key or an AI app), the `channel` it came through and `sandbox`. A customer's id is never given out. A two-way sync skips the events whose actor is its own key.

**Access tokens.** A create call from a caller without an account returns `accessToken`. Send it back as `access_token` (query or body) or as the `x-access-token` header to read, answer or cancel the item; on a signed request, only the header or the body. It is a capability, not an identity: whoever holds it may act on that one item. A person's pass does the same for their own items once the business knows them by it (their first booking with it, or a proven match): with the pass in `Sdi-Pass`, the status, answer, cancel and acknowledge doors need no token.

**Versions.** Item views carry `version`. Send `expected_version` on a transition to be refused with `409 version_conflict` if a colleague, a rule or an agent moved the item first.

**Errors** are RFC 9457 problem documents, `application/problem+json`, with `type` (`https://surfingdog.ai/problems/<code>`), `title`, `status`, `detail`, `code` and, for input problems, `fields` listing `path`, `problem` (`missing` or `invalid`) and `message`. Codes and statuses: `invalid_input` 422, `not_found` 404, `not_allowed` 403, `wrong_state` 409, `unknown_event` 400, `guard_failed` 409, `slot_taken` 409, `version_conflict` 409, `idempotency_mismatch` 422, `unauthorized` 401; for one-time codes `nothing_to_verify` 409, `already_verified` 409, `bad_code` 422, `code_expired` 422, `too_many_attempts` 429; `replayed_signature` 401; `positive_only` 422 for a rule that reads a customer's standing and would refuse, cancel or expire; for a customer's answers `confirm_terms` 409 (also a priced booking or order not yet confirmed), `offer_changed` 409, `offer_expired` 410 (a quote, a time or changes past their deadline) and `no_offer` 409; `confirm_withdrawal` 409 for a withdrawal's statement not yet confirmed; and `confirm_erase` 409 for an erasure not yet confirmed. A refusal a customer may hear through their assistant (`offer_changed`, `offer_expired`, `no_offer`, `slot_taken`, `not_too_soon`) is worded in the business's voice and the customer's language. In MCP the same document comes back in `structuredContent.error` with `isError: true`, and the text names the fields to fix.

**Paging.** List endpoints take `cursor` and `limit` (1 to 100, default 50) and return `items` and `next_cursor`. Pass `next_cursor` back as `cursor` for the next page; it is `null` on the last one.

**Money and time.** Amounts are integers in minor units with an ISO 4217 `currency`. Instants are ISO 8601 with an offset. Durations are minutes.

**Caching.** The profile and the manifest are cacheable for five minutes, services and products for one minute. Everything else is uncacheable.

**Sandbox.** An instance in test mode, a request with the `x-sandbox: 1` header, or a `sandbox.` hostname marks the item as sandbox: same machines, and nothing leaves the inbox. No email goes to the customer or the owner (each is kept as not sent, `test_item`), no network is asked for a key or shown a pass, a one-time code is left on the item as an internal note instead of being emailed, and no receipt is signed. While test mode is on, that holds for every request, real customers' included.

## MCP

Both servers are stateless per request, so a client connects with a plain `POST` per call. Tools carry annotations (`readOnlyHint`, `idempotentHint`, `destructiveHint`) and short server instructions:

- public: start with `get_business_profile`, then `list_services` or `list_products`; for a booking, `check_availability`, then `create_booking` with an idempotency key you generate and keep; keep the access token; when `get_item_status` shows an `offer`, relay `offer.human` as the business said it and call `accept_offer` with its `terms_sha` and `offer_id` only on their clear yes to the summary, otherwise `decline_offer`, or `make_offer` with only what they would change (`suggest_time` for another time; a price of their own goes to a person at the business, and what it proposed still stands); when it waits for details, `provide_details`; a priced `create_booking` or `create_order` answers with the summary to confirm, and is sent again with its `terms_sha` on the person's yes; `withdraw_from_contract` shows the statement first and withdraws on `confirm_withdrawal`, and `request_return` asks to send goods back; `get_item_status` also carries the conversation (`thread`), and a reply marked `automated` was not written by a person. The public server is named after the business, and its answers end with the terms, until when, and the choices, since many assistants read only a tool's text. `accept_offer` without `terms_sha` is not an error: its text is the question to put to the person;
- owner: `list_items` shows what needs a person, `get_item` shows the full story, `transition_item` moves an item with one of the events its view lists, `make_offer` proposes as the item's state allows and `list_offers` shows every offer and answer so far, `reply` speaks to the customer (with a line saying it was sent automatically) and `get_item` shows whether each email went out; never invent facts about availability or prices; give each system its own key; send an idempotency key with every write; a customer's cancellation by email or phone is `record_cancel` with their words, never `cancel_by_business`, and a withdrawal they wrote is `record_withdrawal` naming their message; a return they asked for by email or phone is `open_return`; only a person records a yes to a proposed time given by phone; a customer who asks what the business holds about them gets `export_customer`, one who asks not to be known to booking networks `stop_customer_networks`, and one who asks to be erased is the owner's to erase, in the app; money is the owner's (below).

## Discovery

- `/.well-known/agent-inbox.json`: the [manifest](/docs/manifest/), listing the REST, OpenAPI and MCP doors. The email door is not in it.
- `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp/owner` and `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`: OAuth 2.1 metadata for the owner MCP.
- `/healthz`: `{ "ok": true, "version": "…" }`.
- `/c/{token}`: the page a link in the business's email opens. `GET` shows, `POST` acts; never cached, never indexed, no referrer. The code email's own link opens the page about the booking network for that customer, with the button that switches it off for them.
- `/c/privacy?l=en|pt`: the same page for anyone, in the business's name: which booking networks the instance asks for a first-time customer's code, what they keep, what it is for and how to stop (the code email's link, or a reply to any email).
- `POST /v1/email/inbound`: raw MIME from a mail provider's webhook or a forwarder, with the instance's shared secret in `X-Inbox-Email-Secret`.

## SDK

`@surfingdog/sdk` (MIT, no dependencies) is on npm. For a business's systems it carries the [webhook verifier](/docs/webhooks/) and the event types. For a customer's agent it carries the person's strings (`parseCredential`, `keepPasses`, `sdiPassHeader`, `passFromKey`), request signing (`generateAgentKey`, `signRequest`, and `requestSignInCode`, `signIn` and `delegate` for the one-time setup at a network) and receipts (`verifyReceipt`, `signAck`). Every one is checked against the published vectors. The typed REST client, generated from the OpenAPI document, lands beside them; until then, any OpenAPI client works.
