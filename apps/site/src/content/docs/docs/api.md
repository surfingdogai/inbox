One capability set, several doors. Every operation exists once in the core and is exposed by thin adapters: REST under `/v1` with an OpenAPI 3.1 document, and MCP tools with the same names and the same schemas ([ADR-010](https://github.com/surfingdogai/inbox/blob/main/docs/adr/010-protocols.md)). Whichever door an agent takes, it gets the same item and the same errors.

The live OpenAPI document of our own instance: [inbox.surfingdog.ai/openapi.json](https://inbox.surfingdog.ai/openapi.json). Every instance serves its own at `/openapi.json`.

## Public operations

No authentication. Anonymous callers receive an `accessToken` when they create an item; agents with an agent key (`sdi_agent_…`) are recognised as a stable principal.

| Operation | REST | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `get_business_profile` | `GET /v1/business` | Name, time zone, currency, languages and the item types accepted. |
| `list_services` | `GET /v1/services` | Bookable services with duration and price: a fixed price is for the whole booking unless `price.per` is `person`. Paged. |
| `list_products` | `GET /v1/products` | Orderable products with prices in minor units. Paged, searchable with `q`. |
| `check_availability` | `GET /v1/availability` | Free start times for a service between `from` and `to` (at most 14 days). None that has started, and none that starts within the business's minimum notice (an hour unless it sets another). `party_size` is accepted and does not change the answer yet: a booking takes one place whatever its size. |
| `request_quote` | `POST /v1/quotes` | Ask for a price on something custom. Creates a `quote_request`. |
| `create_booking` | `POST /v1/bookings` | Request a service at a time. Check availability first. Creates a `booking`, at the business's price for a fixed-price service, times `partySize` for a service priced per person. A time that has started is refused (`422` on `payload.startTime`, "That time has passed."); one inside the minimum notice is taken and waits for the business, which does not propose or quote a time inside it either. |
| `create_order` | `POST /v1/orders` | Order products. Creates an `order`, at the business's price for every line that names a product. |
| `get_item_status` | `GET /v1/items/{id}` | The current state of an item you created, in the business's words and language: what it proposed and waits for you to answer (`offer`), who it waits on (`waiting_on`), what you can do next (`next`), a six-character `reference`, and the conversation (`thread`): the business's replies and your messages. |
| `accept_offer` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/accept` | Accept another time the business proposed for a booking, or its quote. Takes the confirm step: send the `terms_sha` your person said yes to. See [Answering the business](#answering-the-business). |
| `decline_offer` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/decline` | Decline it: a booking request whose proposed time you decline is closed, as the customer chose; a declined quote closes the request. Optional `reason`. |
| `suggest_time` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/counter` | Ask for another time than the one proposed: `start_time`, one of the free times `check_availability` lists. The booking goes back to the business, nothing held. |
| `provide_details` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/details` | Answer what the business asked (`details`): an item waiting on your details moves on (`200`); on any other item they are kept as your message (`202`, `waiting_on: "us"`). |
| `cancel_item` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/cancel` | Cancel an item you created. After the business's cancellation window, a confirmed booking is cancelled late where the business records late cancellations, and refused where it does not. |
| `send_message` | `POST /v1/messages` | Start a conversation, or reply on an item you own with `item_id`. A reply on an item waiting on your details answers them: the item moves on. |
| `acknowledge_receipt` | `POST /v1/items/{id}/receipt-ack` | Counter-sign a receipt on your item with your agent's own Ed25519 key, so both sides hold it; or, in a request you sign, name it by `receipt_id`. See [Receipts](/docs/receipts/). |
| `verify_customer` | `POST /v1/customers/verify` | Prove you are a customer the business already knows: six digits go to the address it has for you (`202 {sent_to}`), and sending them back as `code` recognises you (`200 {recognised: "strong"}`). On a test item nothing is sent (`202 {sent_to, test: true}`): the code is left on the item for the business to read. |

Create calls take `payload` (the typed fields, schema.org names in camelCase), an optional `contact` (`name`, `email`, `phone`, `locale`), an optional free-text `message`, and `idempotency_key`.

**The business sets its prices.** A booking of a service priced `fixed`, and every order line that names a product (`productId`, or else `sku`), costs what the business's catalogue says; an order's `totalPrice` is then its lines' prices times their quantities. A different price in the request does not change that: it is kept on the item as `customerStatedPrice` (on the order, and on each line it differs for), for the owner to see, and rules never read it. What the catalogue prices it also names: the service's name, and the line's product's `productId`, `sku` and `name`, replace the request's. A line whose `productId` and `sku` name two different products, and an order whose total is too large to write down exactly, are refused with `422`. A service priced per person (`price.per: "person"`) costs its price times the booking's `partySize` (one person when it has none). A line naming no product the business has, and a service priced `from`, by `quote` or not at all, keep the price written. Those, and a fixed-price service booked for longer than it lasts, wait for the business to price them: nothing confirms or accepts them automatically. A custom job is a `request_quote`.

**Who you carry.** Every public call takes `pass` (a person's passes, space-separated, at most 8) and `key` (a person's key, exchanged once for a pass), or the passes in an `Sdi-Pass` header; a `GET` reads only the header. Never put either in a URL. The inbox presents each to the network that issued it, when that network is switched on, and stores none of them: only a hash of a pass, to recognise it again. Create and status answers carry `identity`:

| Field | |
|---|---|
| `recognised` | `strong` (a customer the business knows, proven), `weak` (the email or phone of one, not proven) or `none`. |
| `passes` | Passes to keep, `[{network, pass}]`: a first booking's pass, or the one a key became. Shown in the answer that made them. |
| `verify` | `available` when a one-time code can prove a weak match; `sent_to`, the masked address, once one went out. |
| `networks` | What happened at each network: `issued`, `presented`, `person_exists`, `pending`, `unknown_pass`, `revoked`, `pass_requires_signature`, `carries_secret`, `unreachable`, `rate_limited`, `not_enabled`, `cannot_sign`. |
| `guide` | `https://surfingdog.ai/for-agents.md` |

A first booking or order with an email and nothing carried asks each network that issues for a key, which is emailed to the customer a day later, alone, with a line about the booking network and a link to a page that explains it, where they can switch it off for themselves; nothing about the booking waits for it. A customer who switched it off (or whom the business switched it off for) is never asked about again: no key, no presentation, no receipt or acknowledgement goes to any network about them, nothing a network said about them is read, and their passes still recognise them, from their hash, with no network call; `identity.networks` is then empty. A network that is slow or down leaves the customer `new` there, never refused. How an agent should use all this is [the agent guide](https://surfingdog.ai/for-agents.md).

**Signed agents.** An agent may sign any request with HTTP Message Signatures (`sdi-agent/1`, Web Bot Auth compatible: its own key in `Sdi-Agent-Key`, or a platform's key named by `Signature-Agent`). An unsigned request is served the same; a signature that does not verify counts as unsigned, and the answer says why in `Sdi-Signature: invalid; reason="…"`. A signed request that changes something is kept once: the same signature again is a retry when it comes from the same client with the same idempotency key (answered as the first time), and `401 replayed_signature` otherwise. A pass reference (`sdpass1_<network>_<id>`) works only in a request signed by a key delegated to it: the inbox forwards that signature to the network, which checks it again, so a signed request must not carry a secret in its URL or its `Sdi-Pass`, nor cover any header but `Content-Digest`, `Content-Type`, `Sdi-Pass` and its key's (`Sdi-Agent-Key` or `Signature-Agent`) (the answer says `carries_secret` and nothing is forwarded). Send an access token in `X-Access-Token` on a signed request. `@surfingdog/sdk` signs requests for you.

## Answering the business

When the business proposes another time for a booking, or sends a quote, it waits for the customer. The status (`GET /v1/items/{id}`, `get_item_status`) then carries:

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| `offer` | `kind` (`time` or `quote`), `terms` (`startTime`, `endTime`, `partySize`, `totalPrice`, and for a quote `lines`, `notes`, `validThrough`, `creates`), `terms_sha` (the fingerprint of those terms), `deadline` (until when you can answer: the proposed start less the business's minimum notice, or the quote's `validThrough`), `obligation_to_pay` and `human`, the terms in the business's words. `null` when nothing waits for an answer. |
| `waiting_on` | `you` (a time or a quote to answer, details asked for, a payment), `us` (the business has it), or `null` once it is closed. |
| `next` | What the customer can do now: `accept_offer`, `decline_offer`, `suggest_time`, `provide_details`, `cancel_item`, `send_message`, each with the business's label. |
| `reference` | Six characters the customer can quote back. |
| `thread` | The conversation, oldest first, the last fifty: `from` (`you`, the customer; `us`, the business), `text`, `at`, and `automated: true` on what a rule or the business's AI sent. The business's internal notes are never in it. |

The item seen by its customer carries none of the business's own flags, in any answer (a create, the status door, an answer to an offer, a cancellation, a message), and a message the business put aside as spam reads as `closed`. Every sentence (`human`, `offer.human`) is the business's, in its language (the customer's `contact.locale`, else the business's first language), with every time in the business's time zone and the zone named.

**The confirm step.** Accepting binds the customer; when the terms carry a price, to pay it. So `accept_offer` without `terms_sha` writes nothing and answers `409 confirm_terms`, whose `details` hold `summary` (what to show your person), `terms_sha` and `obligation_to_pay`. Show them the summary; accept again with that `terms_sha` only on their clear yes. Different terms (the business changed its proposal since you read it) are `409 offer_changed` with the current `offer`; a quote past its validity is `410 offer_expired`; a time that is no longer free is `409 slot_taken`, and one that has started `409 guard_failed` (`not_too_soon`). An accepted time confirms the booking. An accepted quote creates what it was for in the same write, already promised: the booking confirmed at the quoted time with its place held, or the order accepted, returned as `linked`. Nothing is ever booked in the past.

**Emails.** The customer hears from the business by email too, in its name and in the customer's language (English or Portuguese: the customer's `contact.locale`, else the business's first language): an acknowledgement a moment after a booking, order or quote request arrives (a request a rule answers at once gets the answer instead), and an email for every change they need to know of, each with the time in the business's zone and the zone named, the price and the total, until when to answer, and a six-character reference. A reply to one of them lands on the same item: every email about an item names its anchor first in `References`, and the email door matches `In-Reply-To` and `References` against it, against each email's own id, and against the id the mail service gave it. An email no person at the business typed — a rule's, the business's AI's, another system's (an integration key, a shop's connector) — ends with one line: "This reply was sent automatically. Reply to reach a person." A system whose request says a person wrote it (`written_by: "person"` on `reply` or `transition_item`, honoured from an integration key) is not; the owner's AI always is. Every email asks mailboxes not to answer it by themselves (`X-Auto-Response-Suppress: OOF, AutoReply`), and one no person at the business caused says so (`Auto-Submitted: auto-replied` for a reply or the acknowledgement, `auto-generated` otherwise, where the mail service accepts that header; one that refuses it gets the email without it). An out-of-office or a returned email never becomes an item and is never answered: one that answers an item is kept on it as a note, and any other is dropped. The acknowledgement goes to one address at most three times a day, since anyone can make a request with any address; one held back is kept as not sent (`ack_limit`). A test item (`sandbox`) emails nobody, customer or owner: its emails are kept as not sent (`test_item`). The owner is told of new requests at `notifications.ownerEmail`, else at the address the first owner signs in with; with neither, the email is kept as not sent (`no_address`).

The same answers reach a customer by email: the business's email carries **Accept**, **Decline** and **Pick another time** links (or **Send the details**), each opening a small page the instance serves at `/c/{token}` in the business's name and the customer's language. Opening a link only shows the terms; the page's button acts, once. A link names one item and one answer, is signed with the instance's `INBOX_SECRET_KEY`, and lives until the "answer by" date its email shows, and a day more (a quote's, until it lapses and a day more; a details link, 14 days): opened after that date, the page says why it is too late, and offers the free times. Without `INBOX_SECRET_KEY`, or without a public URL (`INBOX_PUBLIC_URL`, else the app URL in the notification settings), the email asks for a reply instead, and the assistant's door works as ever.

## Owner operations

Authenticate with a Bearer token: an owner key, an integration key (see [Keys and scopes](#keys-and-scopes)) or an OAuth 2.1 access token. The owner app uses its session.

| Operation | REST | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `list_items` | `GET /v1/owner/items` | Items newest first. Filters: `type`, `state`, `needs_human`, `mail_failed` (an email that failed, or one to the customer that was never sent: no address, nothing to send from, no mail service, the day's acknowledgements used — a test item's excepted), `open_only` (default true), `sandbox`, `q` (full text, or the six-character reference the customer quotes). Paged. |
| `get_item` | `GET /v1/owner/items/{id}` | One item with its typed fields, events, conversation, receipts, emails and the valid next transitions. `mail` lists every email about it, to the customer and to you, with its `status`: `queued`, `sent` (only once the mail service took it), `retrying` (with `last_error`), `failed`, or `skipped` with a `skip_reason` (`no_address`, `no_sender`, `no_service` when the instance has no mail service and only logged it, `test_item`, `ack_limit` when the address had its acknowledgements for the day); its `body` is the text as sent, with the answer links cut short, so it cannot answer for the customer; a reply in the conversation carries the `delivery` of its email. An email to the customer that failed for good also marks the item as needing you. Each event says who caused it (`by`: `kind`, `id`, and the key's or AI app's `name`) and through which door (`channel`). `customer` says who is asking: `match` (`strong`, `weak`, `none`), `possible` (the customer a weak match may be), `known` and `history` (your own record with them), `persons` (per network that presented them: `tier`, `score`, `kept`, `broken`, `businesses`, `since`, `email_proven`, `unusual_use`, and whether it was with this request or `earlier`), `agent` (how the assistant signed) and `networks_off` (when the customer asked not to be known to booking networks: `since`, `via` — `customer`, `owner` or `erased` — and per network what it already had: `receipts`, `open_promises`, which it records as unclosed nine days after they were due, and `person`; `persons` is then empty). |
| `transition_item` | `POST /v1/owner/items/{id}/transitions` | Fire one of the item's events with its `input`, a `reason` and `expected_version`. `confirm` on a booking in `proposed` books the time you proposed, when the customer said yes by phone, email or in person: only a person may (the owner or staff, not their AI, a rule, an integration key or anything on the owner MCP; `403 not_allowed` with `reason: person_only`). `propose` takes a time that ends after it starts, fits one booking and is free now (`409 slot_taken` otherwise); nothing is held. `record_cancel` records a cancellation the customer asked for (`note`, what they said, required; `askedAt`, when they asked, default now): the customer's, never the business's, judged late or not at the moment they asked. `written_by` says who wrote a note the customer gets (see Emails above). |
| `reply` | `POST /v1/owner/items/{id}/replies` | Reply to the customer, or leave an internal note with `internal: true`. A reply nobody typed — your AI's (or anything through the owner MCP), an integration key's — reaches the customer with the line saying it was sent automatically. `written_by: "person"` from an integration key says its user typed it (no line); `written_by: "automation"` from anyone adds it. |
| — | `GET /v1/owner/customers/{party_id}` | One customer (`party.id` on any of their items): every party that is them (merged, or with the same email address; the same phone number only when they gave no email, and only records that gave none either), how many items, open items, messages and emails, `erased_at`, and `networks_off`. |
| `export_customer` | `GET /v1/owner/customers/{party_id}/export` | Everything the instance holds about them, as one JSON file: their parties, contacts, identities, network ids, agents, and every item with its events, conversation, emails and receipts. Never a pass, a key, an access token or a link. The owner's AI may export. |
| `stop_customer_networks` | `POST /v1/owner/customers/{party_id}/networks-off` | Stop using booking networks for them, as the customer can from their code email: from now on nothing about them goes to any network and nothing a network said about them is read; their bookings, orders and emails are unchanged. It cannot be switched back on. Optional `item_id`, for the note in its history. A network cannot yet be asked to erase what it already has: `networks_off.networks` says what that is. |
| — | `POST /v1/owner/customers/{party_id}/erase` | Erase them, at their request. It cannot be undone, so without `confirm` (or with one that no longer matches: they wrote again since) nothing is erased and the answer is `409 confirm_erase`, whose `details` hold the `summary` and the `confirm` to send. Their names, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses, message text, your notes and the emails about them become a placeholder (`Erased customer`, `[erased]`); what stays is the structure: item types, states, times, amounts and lines, the sequence of events, the receipts, what became of each email. Booking networks are switched off for them for good, and a fingerprint of their email address is kept so that holds if they come back. Only the owner (signed in, or with the owner's own key outside the MCP) or an integration key holding `customers:erase`; never the owner's AI, never through the owner MCP (`403 not_allowed`, `reason: owner_only`), whatever `security.enforceScopes` says. Erasing again answers `already: true`. |
| — | `GET /v1/owner/mail` | Whether email goes out: `service` (a mail service that delivers; without one every email only reaches the log and shows as not sent), `sender` (an address to send from) and `links` (answer links can be signed and opened). Settings shows a banner when one is missing. |
| `get_settings` | `GET /v1/owner/settings` | The settings document and its version. Secrets are never returned: a secret that is set reads `(redacted)`, and `redacted` names its path, such as `email.inboundSecret`. Writing the document back as read, or without it, keeps it. |
| `update_settings` | `PUT /v1/owner/settings` | Change settings: the document you send is merged over the current one, so send only what changes, with `expected_version`. `null` removes a key, so its default applies again. Networks are keyed by origin: `{"networks": {"https://network.example.com": {"enabled": true}}}` adds or switches on one and leaves the others as they are. |
| `get_networks` | `GET /v1/owner/networks` | Each network in settings: on or off, whether it gives first-time customers a key (`issue`), what it gets, whether it has verified this instance, the last ping it took, the last error in a few words, the rules it applies, your own `standing` there (`tier`, `score`, `ranked`, and when it said so) from the last signed ping, what became of that ping's signature (`ping_signature`), and its receipts published, waiting, held, refused and `withheld` (never to be sent: their customers asked not to be known to booking networks). |
| `list_api_keys` | `GET /v1/owner/api-keys` | The owner and integration keys: name, scopes, last use, and every call each made outside its scopes. Never a key itself. |
| `create_api_key` | `POST /v1/owner/api-keys` | A named, scoped, revocable integration key. The key is in this answer and in no other. |
| `revoke_api_key` | `DELETE /v1/owner/api-keys/{id}` | Revoke a key at once and for good. |

Setup (profile, services, products, opening hours, closed days, rules) is under `/v1/owner/profile`, `/services`, `/products`, `/availability` and `/rules`, with the MCP tools `update_profile`, `upsert_service`, `upsert_product`, `set_opening_hours`, `set_closures`, `upsert_rule` and their neighbours. Webhooks, deliveries and the event stream are in [Webhooks](/docs/webhooks/); product feeds (`list_feeds`, `add_feed`, `import_feed_now`, `remove_feed`) in [Feeds](/docs/feeds/). The OpenAPI document lists every owner operation with its query parameters, its response schema and its security.

## Keys and scopes

Give every system that calls the inbox its own key: Zapier, a shop, a till, a form plugin. Create one in the owner app under **Settings → Keys**, or with `POST /v1/owner/api-keys`:

```json
{ "name": "Zapier", "preset": "automation" }
```

The answer holds the key (`sdi_own_…`) once. Send it as `Authorization: Bearer …`. It works on `/v1/owner` and `/mcp/owner` until it is revoked or reaches its optional `expires_at`.

| Preset | Scopes | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `automation` | `inbox:read inbox:write events:read` | Zapier, Make, n8n |
| `shop_sync` | `catalogue:write inbox:read inbox:write events:read` | A shop or a point of sale |
| `calendar_sync` | `availability:write inbox:read inbox:write events:read` | A calendar or a booking tool |
| `read_only` | `inbox:read events:read settings:read` | Reports and dashboards |

The scopes are `inbox:read`, `inbox:write`, `events:read`, `catalogue:write`, `availability:write`, `settings:read`, `settings:write`, `setup:run` (rules), `integrations:write` (webhooks and feeds), `keys:write` and `customers:erase` (erase one customer; in no preset, only the owner can give it, and an AI app is never offered it). OAuth clients ask for the same names, but for `customers:erase`. A key never carries `keys:write`: an integration key cannot mint keys.

**The owner's AI and money.** The owner's AI — an AI app the owner connected over OAuth (actor `owner_ai`), or anything that comes through the owner's MCP, even with a full owner key — confirms and proposes times, and never touches money. It cannot confirm a booking or accept an order whose request held a price the catalogue does not give (`409 guard_failed`, `business_priced`), send a quote, propose a time at a price other than the catalogue's, longer than the service, or on a request that holds a price the catalogue does not give, change a service's or product's price or the business's currency, publish a priced product or service, connect or disconnect a feed, or write, change or switch on a rule that sends a quote or names an amount (`403 not_allowed`, reason `owner_money`). A booking or order it makes through the public doors is priced from the catalogue, as a customer's is. A product, or a service with a price, that it adds is saved unpublished (`active: false`) for the owner to check and publish. Each refusal says so and carries `details.draft_for_owner: true`: the AI leaves the owner a note (`reply` with `internal: true`) with what it suggests. An integration key is another system, not the AI, and is not held this way.

**The owner's AI** — an app connected over OAuth, or anything on the owner MCP, whatever key it holds — reads what customers write, and a customer can write instructions. So nothing that changes where the inbox sends its data is the AI's, whatever its scopes: it gets `403 not_allowed` with `details.reason: "owner_in_person"` and `details.ask_owner: true` for creating or revoking a key, adding, changing, rotating or removing a webhook endpoint (pausing one, `active: false`, is allowed), changing `notifications.ownerEmail`, `notifications.appUrl`, `email.fromAddress`, `email.fromName` or `email.replyTo`, switching webhooks or test mode on, and for everything below. The owner in person does these, and so may an integration key that holds `integrations:write` (endpoints) or `settings:write` (those settings) by name. Only the owner in person changes `email.inboundSecret`, `integrations.webhooks.allowPrivateTargets`, `identity`, `customers.otp` and the `security` section, or switches a network on, lets it issue codes or shares more with it: a network is sent customers' email addresses. A document written back with those unchanged is accepted and leaves them as they are. `security.aiMayCreateKeys` is retired and grants nothing. A reply or a transition note from the AI that names another customer's email address or phone number, or carries a key or secret, is refused too (`details.reason: "would_leak"`). What customers wrote reaches the AI after each tool's own text, in a block drawn with a random boundary, and in `structuredContent.untrusted_content`.

**Scopes are logged first.** In this release a call outside a key's scopes, or outside an AI app's granted scopes, still goes through, and it is recorded: `list_api_keys` shows each key's calls outside its scopes, with the scope it needed, and the same for AI apps. The owner can refuse them now with **Refuse calls outside a key's scopes** (`security.enforceScopes`); a later release turns that on for everyone. An AI that sets things up should ask for the scopes it needs when it connects.

**Limits.** An integration key has a generous bucket of its own, about ten calls a second, so a loop between the inbox and another system cannot run for ever. The owner's own session, keys and AI are not limited.

## Conventions

**Idempotency.** Every write takes an idempotency key: in the `Idempotency-Key` header or as `idempotency_key` in the body on REST, as the `idempotency_key` argument on MCP. That includes the owner's setup writes (services, products, hours, rules, settings, webhooks, feeds, keys). Same key and same request: the stored answer is replayed with status `200` and `Idempotent-Replayed: true`, and nothing is done twice. Same key and a different request: `422 idempotency_mismatch`. The owner's and the owner's AI's keys are scoped to the business, so a retry through MCP of a request first sent through REST is the same request; an integration key's keys are its own, so two systems that pick the same key never collide; a customer's keys are scoped to the customer and the door. An answer that carries a secret (a signing secret, a key) is stored sealed, and a replay shows the secret only to whoever made the first request. Keys are kept for 30 days: they are for retries, not a record.

**Who did it.** Every event, in the history, the [event stream and webhooks](/docs/webhooks/), carries `actor` (`kind`: `owner`, `owner_ai`, `integration`, `connector`, `rule`, `system`, `customer_agent` or `customer_human`; `id`; and `name` for a key or an AI app), the `channel` it came through and `sandbox`. A customer's id is never given out. A two-way sync skips the events whose actor is its own key.

**Access tokens.** A create call from a caller without an account returns `accessToken`. Send it back as `access_token` (query or body) or as the `x-access-token` header to read, answer or cancel the item; on a signed request, only the header or the body. It is a capability, not an identity: whoever holds it may act on that one item. A person's pass does the same for their own items once the business knows them by it (their first booking with it, or a proven match): with the pass in `Sdi-Pass`, the status, answer, cancel and acknowledge doors need no token.

**Versions.** Item views carry `version`. Send `expected_version` on a transition to be refused with `409 version_conflict` if a colleague, a rule or an agent moved the item first.

**Errors** are RFC 9457 problem documents, `application/problem+json`, with `type` (`https://surfingdog.ai/problems/<code>`), `title`, `status`, `detail`, `code` and, for input problems, `fields` listing `path`, `problem` (`missing` or `invalid`) and `message`. Codes and statuses: `invalid_input` 422, `not_found` 404, `not_allowed` 403, `wrong_state` 409, `unknown_event` 400, `guard_failed` 409, `slot_taken` 409, `version_conflict` 409, `idempotency_mismatch` 422, `unauthorized` 401; for one-time codes `nothing_to_verify` 409, `already_verified` 409, `bad_code` 422, `code_expired` 422, `too_many_attempts` 429; `replayed_signature` 401; `positive_only` 422 for a rule that reads a customer's standing and would refuse, cancel or expire; for a customer's answers `confirm_terms` 409, `offer_changed` 409, `offer_expired` 410 and `no_offer` 409; and `confirm_erase` 409 for an erasure not yet confirmed. A refusal a customer may hear through their assistant (`offer_changed`, `offer_expired`, `no_offer`, `slot_taken`, `not_too_soon`) is worded in the business's voice and the customer's language. In MCP the same document comes back in `structuredContent.error` with `isError: true`, and the text names the fields to fix.

**Paging.** List endpoints take `cursor` and `limit` (1 to 100, default 50) and return `items` and `next_cursor`. Pass `next_cursor` back as `cursor` for the next page; it is `null` on the last one.

**Money and time.** Amounts are integers in minor units with an ISO 4217 `currency`. Instants are ISO 8601 with an offset. Durations are minutes.

**Caching.** The profile and the manifest are cacheable for five minutes, services and products for one minute. Everything else is uncacheable.

**Sandbox.** An instance in test mode, a request with the `x-sandbox: 1` header, or a `sandbox.` hostname marks the item as sandbox: same machines, and nothing leaves the inbox. No email goes to the customer or the owner (each is kept as not sent, `test_item`), no network is asked for a key or shown a pass, a one-time code is left on the item as an internal note instead of being emailed, and no receipt is signed. While test mode is on, that holds for every request, real customers' included.

## MCP

Both servers are stateless per request, so a client connects with a plain `POST` per call. Tools carry annotations (`readOnlyHint`, `idempotentHint`, `destructiveHint`) and short server instructions:

- public: start with `get_business_profile`, then `list_services` or `list_products`; for a booking, `check_availability`, then `create_booking` with an idempotency key you generate and keep; keep the access token; when `get_item_status` shows an `offer`, tell your person `offer.human` as the business wrote it and call `accept_offer` with its `terms_sha` only on their clear yes, otherwise `decline_offer` or `suggest_time`; when it waits for details, `provide_details`; `get_item_status` also carries the conversation (`thread`), and a reply marked `automated` was not written by a person. The public server is named after the business, and its answers end with the terms, until when, and the choices, since many assistants read only a tool's text. `accept_offer` without `terms_sha` is not an error: its text is the question to put to the person;
- owner: `list_items` shows what needs a person, `get_item` shows the full story, `transition_item` moves an item with one of the events its view lists, `reply` speaks to the customer (with a line saying it was sent automatically) and `get_item` shows whether each email went out; never invent facts about availability or prices; give each system its own key; send an idempotency key with every write; a customer's cancellation by email or phone is `record_cancel` with their words, never `cancel_by_business`; only a person records a yes to a proposed time given by phone; a customer who asks what the business holds about them gets `export_customer`, one who asks not to be known to booking networks `stop_customer_networks`, and one who asks to be erased is the owner's to erase, in the app; money is the owner's (below).

## Discovery

- `/.well-known/agent-inbox.json`: the [manifest](/docs/manifest/), listing the REST, OpenAPI and MCP doors. The email door is not in it.
- `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp/owner` and `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`: OAuth 2.1 metadata for the owner MCP.
- `/healthz`: `{ "ok": true, "version": "…" }`.
- `/c/{token}`: the page a link in the business's email opens. `GET` shows, `POST` acts; never cached, never indexed, no referrer. The code email's own link opens the page about the booking network for that customer, with the button that switches it off for them.
- `/c/privacy?l=en|pt`: the same page for anyone, in the business's name: which booking networks the instance asks for a first-time customer's code, what they keep, what it is for and how to stop (the code email's link, or a reply to any email).
- `POST /v1/email/inbound`: raw MIME from a mail provider's webhook or a forwarder, with the instance's shared secret in `X-Inbox-Email-Secret`.

## SDK

`@surfingdog/sdk` (MIT, no dependencies) is on npm. For a business's systems it carries the [webhook verifier](/docs/webhooks/) and the event types. For a customer's agent it carries the person's strings (`parseCredential`, `keepPasses`, `sdiPassHeader`, `passFromKey`), request signing (`generateAgentKey`, `signRequest`, and `requestSignInCode`, `signIn` and `delegate` for the one-time setup at a network) and receipts (`verifyReceipt`, `signAck`). Every one is checked against the published vectors. The typed REST client, generated from the OpenAPI document, lands beside them; until then, any OpenAPI client works.
